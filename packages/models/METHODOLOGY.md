# Analytical engine: implementation and evidence boundaries

The model package runs the same deterministic calculations in the browser and API. `calculateImpact(place, scenario, evidence?)` returns revision-bound metrics. Timestamps are metadata; numerical results and provenance are deterministic. Model version: `0.7.0`.

## Baseline and units

- `Place` contains source geometry. `Scenario` contains overrides, an editable assumptions profile, and a frozen `baselineAssumptions` profile. Both sides are evaluated at the selected forecast horizon. Physical building counts do not grow automatically.
- `Building.areaSqm` is footprint area. New complete geometry derives this value from all components minus holes; legacy entered areas remain unchanged. An explicit program supplies gross floor area; legacy buildings use footprint × floors. Convert square meters to square feet once where a coefficient requires it. Parks and vacant land have no building floor area. Parking structures contribute gross floor area but receive no default trip or parking demand.
- `height`, `floors`, `units`, `jobs`, and `parkingSpaces` accept explicit unknown (`null`) values. Zero remains a supplied or reviewed zero. Null requires per-field missing provenance; a numeric value cannot retain missing metadata. Imported values record their source field, raw value, units and supplied/derived/assumed status. Estimates require an explicit reviewed assumption; ingestion does not manufacture quantities from a blank cell.
- Polygon areas use a spherical ring calculation in WGS84. Road distances use great-circle segment distances. Parcel FAR requires explicit land boundaries/areas and building associations; a footprint cannot substitute for a parcel. User planning boundaries do not establish recorded cadastral parcels.

## Missing quantities and imported evidence (0.7.0)

Format 8 stores optional per-attribute provenance and bounded import/source-rights records without rewriting prior source bytes. A manifest retains upload/member hashes and sizes, selected layer/entities, source CRS and axis order, coordinate operation and converter identity, normalized geometry hash, the declarative mapping recipe, and explicit exclusions. Rights are the uploader's attestation for private storage, analysis, display, export and history retention; public redistribution is a separate permission, not proof of a license review. Append imports require new identifiers; matched imports require existing identifiers (and the same parcel/block kind). Each import remains one reversible command.

The model keeps known contributions distinct from complete totals. An unknown required attribute withholds the dependent full baseline/proposal value and its comparison. Additive metrics may expose an explicitly labeled known subtotal and missing building IDs; a ratio, net fiscal balance or NPV does not present a partial number as a valid subtotal. If every contributor is unknown, the known subtotal is also unavailable. Arithmetic helpers do not turn null into zero. Physical geometry, footprint/parcel union coverage and road measures remain available independently of missing building heights, floors or program counts. Missing height only prevents a height-rule check; it does not prevent footprint analysis.

Explicit programs keep numeric component homes, jobs and declared GFA authoritative; their top-level mirrors must agree. A known explicit program can therefore support those metrics even when massing height/floor count is unknown. A supplied assessed value independently supports property valuation. A legacy mixed-use record with unknown floors cannot establish even its assumed retail floor. Program adoption that would reinterpret unknown floors/homes/jobs is blocked until those inputs are reviewed; an explicit typed-default replacement stamps its assumptions and remains reversible. Geometry edits never invent missing quantities.

Building-weighted activity allocations remain unknown if relevant weights are missing; known buildings are not renormalized to conceal the gap. Explicit area-wide count targets can still be displayed, but proportional demand requiring an unknown physical denominator remains unavailable. An unchanged unknown building count cancels only when calculating an incremental capacity difference against an independent reference; editing an unknown count, adding or removing an unknown-count building does not supply a numeric delta. Matching published population can support baseline resident-service cost independently of mapped housing completeness. Zero configured service/tax coefficients retain a true zero contribution. Public fiscal cash-flow rows preserve null for dependent revenues/costs and withhold complete net balances.

Parcel GFA, homes and jobs follow the same completeness rule for wholly contained buildings; footprint union coverage does not require those quantities. Straddlers still make whole-building totals unavailable. Frozen retail and workforce captures retain their own values, missing-data rules, source inventories and method versions. An eight-scenario golden regression compares every metric and every annual fiscal flow against the preceding all-numeric engine; numeric legacy results are unchanged.

## Implemented directly from geometry and assumptions

Housing counts/mix, occupied units, housing-derived residents, gross density, jobs-housing balance, rent-only income screening, direct jobs/payroll, generated daily trips, screening VMT, parking demand/supply/balance, road/lane/bike-lane miles, operating fiscal cash flows, incentive payback, gross floor area, footprint area, geometry-backed parcel FAR and lot coverage, mapped park area, supplied height-limit conflicts, and screening transportation CO₂.

Default coefficients are **illustrative product assumptions**, not Phoenix observations, ITE coefficients, or licensed regional model inputs. `tripRatesConfirmed` and `fiscalInputsConfirmed` default to false. Their results remain visibly incomplete until both scenario and baseline profiles are reviewed. Manually confirming a profile does not constitute external model validation.

Default trip coefficients are daily vehicle-trip ends per occupied dwelling or per 1,000 commercial square feet at a reference drive share of 80%. Explicit component overrides also support dwelling, job, and bed denominators. Mode changes scale this reference demand linearly. Legacy mixed-use assumes residential demand plus one full footprint floor of retail; explicit programs calculate their actual entered use components separately. No pass-by/internal-capture reduction is assumed. The result is not an assigned traffic count, origin/destination model, calibrated peak delay, or level of service.

Fiscal results are operating estimates in constant dollars. Annual service costs scale with residents/jobs; retail sales capacity scales with forecast growth. Assessed values remain constant unless edited. Scenario abatements apply only positive added property value, evaluated using the same valuation profile on both sides, rather than the entire existing tax base. Grants are paid once at time zero. Recurring cash flows are discounted at year end. Payback compares cumulative incremental net cash flow against the baseline. A null payback with “not reached” is a valid modeled outcome.

The fiscal model currently excludes capital-project schedules, fee schedules, assessment caps/classifications, tax-district overlaps, exemptions, and detailed retail displacement. Generic fiscal defaults are not a claim about a real city's retained tax revenue.

### Cited fiscal studies and paired alternatives (`fiscal-study-1.0.0`)

Project format 12 optionally retains an immutable fiscal profile with nine required coefficients, per-field published/derived/authored provenance, complete source citations and declared file receipts, jurisdiction applicability, fiscal year, separate constant-dollar year, real discount basis, taxable-assessed valuation compatibility and service-allocation notes. These are reviewed inputs, not independently authenticated tax or budget determinations. Published rates per assessed value cannot silently be applied to market values. Average budget allocations are not marginal development costs; resident and workplace allocation assumptions must avoid counting the same expense twice. No local coefficients are manufactured when a profile is absent.

Creating a fiscal study makes a **new project** from the original Place and selected current proposal. It initializes the fiscal coefficients, jurisdiction name and review flag on the new frozen reference and proposal while preserving all other assumptions separately on each side. It starts empty history/undo stacks; original history remains in the source project. Lineage hashes bind the source project, selected scenario/revision and history. Copied workforce, accessibility, traffic and flood references become imported evidence without altering captured numerical content. Earlier projects, publications, baseline geometry and native artifacts remain unchanged.

The proposal retains an explicit input review and up to 12 named paired sensitivity cases. Changes to reviewed monetary/demographic assumptions make that review stale. A renewed review records its actor, time and notes; deviations remain labeled authored overrides, so original profile citations never validate changed values. Geometry and offer changes are responsive inputs and do not themselves revise coefficient citations. Cases can change baseline and proposal coefficients separately; they are authored alternatives, not probabilities, confidence intervals, optimized bounds or independently validated forecasts.

Each case returns three trajectories: the frozen reference, the same proposed development without an incentive offer, and that development with the offer. This comparison does not predict whether incentives cause development. All include a separate time-zero row and 20 year-end operating rows. The valuation date is the start of the selected scenario horizon, **not automatically today**. The horizon advances existing growth assumptions once; it does not supply a construction, absorption or tax-reassessment schedule. The grant is charged once at time zero. Assessed values stay constant; the existing shared growth assumption applies to sales and operating costs. Abatement applies only to positive added assessed value, valuing existing property under the proposal's valuation assumptions to avoid treating an assumption change as new development.

Totals, discounted totals and paired incremental rows preserve unknown quantities; a partial net balance or NPV is never called complete. Known offer cost is calculated directly from the grant and abatement components, even if an unrelated operating input is missing. Payback distinguishes zero actual offer cost, first recovered year, not reached within 20 years, and unavailable incremental flows. Overall results remain incomplete if any named case lacks required quantities. Complete assumption sets, missing building identifiers, clock and exact input/profile hashes accompany results and exports. Without a fiscal study, the legacy `0.7.0` calculations and optional-field absence are unchanged, including their existing annual-row convention.

Since version 0.2.0, the model can anchor existing resident-service costs to an ACS population estimate only when an explicit whole-ZCTA study geography matches the demographic source. Added/removed dwelling capacity contributes incremental residents under the scenario's occupancy and household-size assumptions; population multipliers and growth remain explicit scenarios. Partial study areas retain housing-derived resident costs and show whole-ZCTA statistics only as context. This legacy path does not spatially prorate population. The separately adopted activity layer below supports explicitly modeled neighborhood allocations. Census housing counts do not replace mapped dwelling units or housing-supported residents. Published population margins are disclosed but not propagated into fiscal totals. The historical ACS period remains visible; it is not relabeled as a current-year count. See the [Census provider methodology](../../apps/api/src/data/README.md).

## Explicit population, employment, and household-vehicle scenarios

Version 0.5.0 adds an optional `Scenario.activity` layer. Its absence preserves the previous calculation path and captured source bytes. Adoption previews the current scenario as explicit counts; it does not rewrite buildings or the baseline. References distinguish a matching published whole-ZCTA population estimate, a housing-supported or entered-building-job estimate, and an explicitly sourced user count with year and universe. The unqualified legacy `Place.population` is not promoted to observed evidence. There is no connected published workplace-employment adapter. A supplied forecast remains user-provided evidence, not a validated regional model.

An absolute target is the count **at the selected horizon**. A percentage is a percentage of the captured year-zero reference: 125% means reference × 1.25, including for a ten-year scenario. Neither target receives horizon growth a second time. Baseline comparison uses the frozen reference × baseline growth to that horizon. Without an explicit target, the natural population/employment count is `(reference + changed building capacity) × scenario horizon growth`; population also retains the existing multiplier when no target replaces it. Housing capacity uses entered homes × occupancy × household size. Effective residents and employment are reported separately from unchanged housing capacity and entered building jobs. Decimal estimates are retained rather than rounded into false exact counts.

Neighborhoods are full disjoint Polygon/MultiPolygon planning boundaries, including holes. A brush is clipped to the exact study area and unclaimed space; precise overlapping boundaries are rejected. The chosen allocation method is explicit: building-weighted (homes for population, entered jobs for employment), uniform land area, or unallocated. Building-weighted counts split a straddling building's weight by clipped footprint fraction in a common local plane. This is a spatial allocation assumption, not observed neighborhood demographics, and does not alter the whole-building association rules used for parcel FAR. Separate overlapping source buildings retain separate entered programs; their potential duplication remains a source limitation.

The displayed global target is the count **before** neighborhood exceptions. Final effective count is `global + Σ(local target − allocated global count in that neighborhood)`. Numerically, the engine evaluates the equivalent `global × unoverridden weight + Σ(local targets)` using deterministic compensated sums, avoiding cancellation when all targets are zero. Only an impossible coverage-weight overshoot above one within `8 × Number.EPSILON × max(1, number of overrides)` is normalized to one; positive uncovered remainders, however small, are retained. Unknown allocations are never normalized into known counts. Areas outside edited neighborhoods retain their allocated counts; the engine does not rebalance them to force the original global target. Array order does not change totals. Local percentages use the corresponding allocated captured reference. Changing physical building quantities can change building-weighted allocation; this follows the selected spatial assumption, not a hidden relocation operation. Zero-weight areas remain unallocated unless the user deliberately selects another method. A known local absolute count is retained even when an unknown remainder makes the whole-study total unavailable. With no neighborhoods, no spatial allocation is required. A ready count means its numerical accounting is complete; its source may still be inferred, historical, or user-entered.

The `+25% population` preset sets 125% of the selected reference. The `+10-year regional forecast` preset requires a sourced forecast with an explicit ten-year span, baseline/target years and geography; matching-area totals are a user assertion, while a regional ratio is explicitly applied as a local sensitivity. No forecast values are fabricated. The plant-closure preset removes an entered number of jobs from a selected neighborhood's known employment count without deleting the building or altering its floor area. Applying a preset, source changes, neighborhood changes, and an optional horizon change can be committed together as one reversible activity command.

Demand response requires an explicit reviewed selection. Residential population response reweights mapped residential demand by effective-to-natural population ratios. Employment response reweights explicit job-denominator demand and default office/industrial/warehouse floor-area demand; bed, retail floor-area, and other customer/visitor denominators remain unchanged. Dwelling/occupied-dwelling parking retains its physical denominator. Explicit job-based parking responds regardless of component use, including residential staff jobs; eligible workplace floor-area parking can also respond. Parking removes the horizon-growth term from its employment-response denominator because, unlike trips, it has no later growth multiplication: a fixed 100 effective jobs at one space/job requires 100 spaces at either horizon 0 or 10. Missing or zero denominators supporting positive activity produce unavailable affected demand, not fabricated zero trips. A reviewed zero target explicitly sets eligible demand to zero even with a zero or unknown reference; that exception is an authored no-activity choice, not a calibrated elasticity. These are proportional planning sensitivities, not calibrated travel behavior, mode choice, or traffic assignment.

Household vehicles available are a separate stock. Optional frozen ACS B25046 evidence uses the direct published aggregate and its margin of error for occupied housing units; exact ZCTA ID, 2020 boundary vintage, same-area relationship, and available estimate status must match. Suppressed/missing values stay unavailable, while a published zero remains zero. An adopted supplement is stored on the scenario without mutating the captured Place. Vehicles kept at home are not a registered fleet, AADT, daily trips, or animated agents. Population/employment changes do not automatically grow this stock. A selected stock-to-reference ratio **replaces** the residential population response; it never multiplies both ratios. A positive absolute vehicle target without a usable reference cannot establish a trip response; a reviewed zero target uses the explicit zero-response exception above. See the [vehicle provider and source limitations](../../apps/api/src/data/README.md).

Effective employment changes direct jobs, wage-based payroll and compatible supplied multiplier results. Effective population/employment drive operating service costs; park area per resident uses effective population. Fiscal service counts start at the chosen horizon and then receive only year-1 continuation growth, avoiding double application of the horizon. Property value, dwelling supply, floor area, retail sales capacity, parking supply and physical road measures remain unchanged unless their own inputs change. Zero service coefficients contribute zero even when the corresponding count is unknown; nonzero coefficients require that count. Published margins of error are retained as evidence but are not propagated through these deterministic sensitivities.

Geometry admission is bounded: at most 100 neighborhoods, 500 ring points plus closure, 5,000 vertices per feature, 50,000 total vertices, and 50,000 building/neighborhood broad-phase candidates. Limits are checked independently of cached intersections; repeated attempts cannot bypass them by warming the cache. Over-complex work is rejected explicitly with `ACTIVITY_COMPLEXITY`, not simplified, truncated, or evaluated from a subset. Activity changes increment scenario revision and invalidate older revision-bound routing/safety evidence. Current, history and undone states retain full inputs and sources under project format 5; undo does not downgrade the format. Untouched formats 1–4 and source hashes remain supported.

Neighborhood calibration, observed occupied-job inputs, verified regional forecasts, household travel surveys, redistribution models, routing/assignment and external forecasting validation remain outstanding. The activity controls are useful authored sensitivities, not a substitute for those full-product requirements.

## Category retail demand–sales workbench

Version 0.6.0 adds an optional persisted `Scenario.retail` study. It retains user-entered annual demand, local establishment sales, optional sales-per-establishment benchmarks, per-category proposed capacity and capture/displacement assumptions. Current, history, undone and command-only payloads retain the same inputs, source records and rights declarations under project format 6. One `scenario.retail` command applies/removes the study; branch and undo preserve it without altering captured Place data or downgrading the project format. With no persisted study, previous calculations and the legacy optional scalar `ModelEvidence.retail` path remain unchanged. The legacy pack is not silently converted into categorized evidence; a persisted workbench takes precedence when both are supplied.

Every study declares one geography, its relation to the mapped study area, reference year, USD dollar year, classification/version and reviewed scenario horizon. Users must confirm that the measures have matching annual periods, prices, geography and category definitions. This is an attestation, not an automated validation of the source methodology. A broader trade-area estimate remains labeled as context; it is never relabeled or spatially prorated to ZIP totals. Changing the scenario horizon withholds proposal retail results until the study is reviewed and reapplied; baseline evidence remains visible. Explicit scenario demand is an annual amount at the reviewed horizon; null means retain baseline annual demand. No inflation, population, household, employment or horizon-growth multiplier is automatically applied.

Establishment industries and commodity expenditure categories are not interchangeable. NAICS categories require 2–6 digit codes with no duplicates or parent/child overlap; custom categories require an explicit mutually-exclusive-category declaration. An external commodity-to-industry mapping must be documented in the supplied source rather than inferred from a category name. The Census retail definitions distinguish establishment-based sales and current-dollar reporting; collected sales taxes are excluded from retail sales. See the [official Census retail definitions](https://www.census.gov/econ_file/retail/definitions.html). Public BLS Consumer Expenditure data do not directly provide ZIP/block/tract spending estimates, so a national or metropolitan coefficient must not masquerade as observed local demand; see [BLS geographic coverage](https://www.bls.gov/cex/csxgeography.htm).

For each category, let `D` be explicit scenario demand (or unchanged reference demand), `S` reference annual establishment sales, `C` additional prospective annual sales capacity, `r` capture share, and `d` displacement share. The workbench computes:

1. Signed baseline gap = reference demand − reference sales.
2. Eligible scenario gap = max(D − S, 0).
3. Gross prospective captured sales = min(eligible gap × r, C).
4. Displaced local sales = gross prospective captured sales × d.
5. Net new studied-category sales = gross prospective captured sales × (1 − d).
6. Scenario sales = S + net new sales; scenario gap = D − scenario sales.

`C` is nonnegative additional capacity entered separately for each category. It is not total added retail floor area reused in every row. This model does not automatically assign building programs to categories, model closures, reduce existing-store sales when demand falls, or assume additional visitor/inbound spending. Full displacement produces no net local sales gain. A zero capacity or capture share produces a known zero addition even if baseline demand is missing; it does not make a missing gap known. A negative signed gap is sales surplus relative to supplied resident demand, not proof of consumer inflows, store oversupply or measured resident spending leakage. All-establishment sales do not identify where resident purchases occurred.

Available zero amounts are distinct from missing or suppressed values. Suppression is retained with its source and no imputed zero. Optional establishment equivalents use `max(gap, 0) ÷ positive annual sales-per-establishment benchmark` separately by category, retaining fractions. They are revenue equivalents, not a prediction of business openings, a count of viable sites, or evidence of available labor. Missing benchmarks withhold only their equivalents, not otherwise known monetary results. The complete aggregate for any field is unavailable if any studied category lacks that field; a separately labeled known subtotal sums only available rows and remains null when none are known. Monetary completeness excludes optional benchmarks. Deterministic compensated sums reduce order-dependent cancellation, including signed category gaps. Aggregates cover the **studied categories only**, never an unsupported total for the full retail economy.

Every referenced demand/sales/benchmark/assumption source must exist without conflicting metadata under the same source ID. Each source has a user declaration authorizing storage, analysis, authorized-project sharing and private export. Public redistribution is a separate declaration covering raw values and attribution; a private declaration in the current pack blocks publication, including an unused retained rights statement. These declarations do not verify ownership or grant a vendor license. Private authorized exports remain possible. User-entered packs carry no claim of independent verification, and no paid data provider is connected by this workbench.

Retail results do not automatically flow into fiscal sales-tax estimates, job totals, building floor area, population or traffic demand. This prevents double counting against the existing building-capacity fiscal model. Authorized category datasets, verified trade-area geography, observed buyer-origin evidence, validated demand mapping, retail-site feasibility, full workforce/occupation sheds and external validation remain requirements of the complete product plan.

## Explicit building programs and complete footprints

Version 0.4.0 supports either the unchanged legacy exterior-ring `footprint` or a complete Polygon/MultiPolygon `geometry`, never two competing authoritative shapes. Holes and detached components remain part of the same feature. New geometry must stay within the exact study area; authored rings allow 500 points plus closure and 5,000 total vertices. Unchanged captured detail retains settings-only compatibility. Overlapping building features are disclosed rather than rejected as overlapping parcels: each feature's program contributes to totals, while parcel coverage unions their footprints once. Geometry may not establish whether overlapping source features are duplicate buildings or intentional parts.

An explicit `program` declares gross floor area, common area, and up to 64 use components. Component floor areas plus common area must equal the declared total. Each component records its concrete use, homes, jobs, optional residential subtype and beds, and source references. Top-level homes/jobs are validated mirrors of the component totals, not additional quantities. Broad land use follows the sole component use or `mixed-use` for multiple uses; common-only programs retain their supplied classification. Zero remains an entered zero. Geometry resizing, movement, or rotation does not scale homes/jobs or rewrite an explicit program. Mismatches between declared GFA and footprint × floors are disclosed; the map shows uniform-height massing rather than surveyed floor-by-floor design.

Trips and parking demand sum component quantities using their explicit denominators: dwelling, occupied dwelling, 1,000 square feet, job, or bed. Per-bed coefficients require an actual entered bed count; a missing count is not zero. Without an override, residential components use household coefficients and other nonparking components use floor-area coefficients from the scenario profile. Residential trip demand receives the population multiplier; the established growth/drive-share/vehicle multipliers then apply to total generated demand. Rate groups, denominator quantities, coefficients, and source IDs appear in metric inputs. No shared-parking or mixed-use internal-capture credit is assumed. Parking supply is entered once per building. Parking components and common area generate no independent default trips or parking demand, but remain part of total floor area and default assessed value. Only explicit retail space contributes to retail sales-capacity calculations.

The typed library distinguishes townhomes and hospitals while keeping their broad land-use categories compatible with existing assumptions. Its geometry, capacity, and massing defaults are illustrative, not observed dwelling occupancy, employment, licensed beds, medical demand, or development entitlements. Library defaults retain their own inference source; user-authored geometry and changed programs have separate provenance. Legacy mixed-use can be explicitly adopted using the current scenario's trip assumptions, after which the component allocations are reviewable. A conversion preview either retains the entered program or deliberately applies typed defaults; the latter retains identity, footprint, and recorded address/parcel/zoning/valuation metadata while replacing the disclosed development quantities.

Extended buildings require project format 3 in the baseline, current alternatives, and retained undo/history. Untouched format-1/2 objects and captured source hashes are not rewritten. Undo cannot downgrade a format-3 project. General internal feature IDs reject reserved object-property names, including raw edit-record keys; external source feature references remain bounded free text. Model methods, project format, baseline identity, and storage envelope versions are independent.

## Street geometry and network measures

The proposal road set is materialized from the captured baseline plus scenario overrides. `road.upsert` adds or replaces a street; `road.remove` records a removal without changing the baseline. Undo/redo and baseline restoration retain the captured source geometry and advance the scenario revision. Saved projects, published snapshots, and exports use applied geometry, not an unfinished editor draft.

Open road miles sum great-circle centerline segment lengths and divide meters by 1,609.344. Open lane miles multiply each open street's length by its entered lane count. Bike-lane miles count the centerline length of open streets flagged with a bike lane, without multiplying by the number of lanes or asserting protection or continuity. Closed and removed roads contribute no proposal open-road, lane, or bike-lane miles; the baseline comparison remains intact. These are geometric measures, not capacity, traffic, or accessibility predictions.

New streets use the `user-road-proposal` source and start with two lanes, 25 mph, two-way travel, and `estimated: true`, with no AADT. These are editable proposal assumptions, not observed conditions. `geometrySourceId` records authored alignment separately from the original `sourceId` and attribute provenance. The street editor clears a retained AADT when the alignment changes. Road metrics cite road geometry and attribute sources; lane miles remain incomplete when either side includes estimated attributes.

Changed paths are limited to 500 points, at least one meter total length, and at least five centimeters between consecutive points. They must remain wholly within the exact study area, including holes and separate components. Duplicate/backtracking segments and self-crossing paths are rejected; a simple closed loop is allowed. Unchanged captured paths retain their existing schema allowance and can receive settings-only edits without being retroactively rejected by new authoring rules. These checks establish valid proposal geometry, not road-design compliance.

Optional snapping aligns a draft point to a nearby existing street vertex; it does not insert junctions or infer connectivity, grade separation, turn restrictions, or a routable graph. Intersection design, stop/crosswalk/roundabout authoring, curb-parking effects, signal timing response, and transit assignment remain unfinished. Drawing or removing a road does not manufacture traffic counts, rerouting, congestion, or mode shifts. Revision-bound routing evidence becomes stale after an edit and must be replaced by a matching regional run before it can support accessibility results.

## Parcel geometry, associations, and land measures

Version 0.3.0 adds optional captured `Place.parcels` and scenario `parcelEdits`. A feature is a `parcel` or a `block`, with full Polygon/MultiPolygon geometry, source references, and operation lineage. `materializeParcels` applies additions, reshapes, and removals to the captured baseline without mutating it. Draw, reshape, remove, split, assembly, and a reviewed GeoJSON import each commit as one reversible command; drafts do not contribute to results. A removal affects the boundary only, not its buildings.

Area is derived from the complete geometry using Turf's spherical WGS84 area calculation, subtracting holes and summing components; a client-supplied area cache is never trusted. Authored/imported rings allow 500 distinct editing points plus a closing point, 5,000 vertices per feature, and 5,000 parcel/block features per scenario. Same-kind boundaries may touch but cannot materially overlap; a block may overlap parcel land. Bounding-box filtering and a 50,000-candidate intersection budget bound overlap validation. Complex input is rejected explicitly, never simplified or partly retained.

Subdivision uses the two complementary halfplanes of an extended straight line. Each resulting connected component becomes a new lot, with all original holes retained or opened into the cut boundary. A tangent/no-cut, a component below 1 m², invalid geometry, or excessive complexity is rejected. Assembly unions two or more features of the same kind; disconnected land remains an explicitly labeled MultiPolygon. Both operations verify that the output covers the original land without introduced overlaps, missing areas, or filled holes. Conservation, containment, and overlap checks measure clipping residues in a translated local plane, consistent with the straight longitude/latitude edges used by polygon clipping. Their numerical area tolerance is the greater of 0.001 m² and one billionth of the relevant land area. Displayed land areas retain the spherical calculation; inserting a collinear point on a sloped edge can change that approximation slightly, so it is not used as the topology-conservation oracle. Coordinates are never snapped or buffered to hide gaps. They do not establish surveying accuracy, legal subdivision, access, or buildability.

`calculateParcelContext` matches **whole structural footprints**, excluding park/vacant features. A wholly contained building contributes its explicit declared program gross floor area and component homes/jobs, or the legacy entered footprint area × floors and entered counts when no program is present. A footprint crossing the selected boundary is listed as a straddler: full floor area, FAR, homes, and jobs become unavailable rather than being assigned by centroid or proportional overlap. Reported `Building.parcelId` values are retained separately; spatial conflicts are disclosed rather than silently rewriting those IDs. Inferred building programs remain incomplete, and missing mapped structures are not proof of vacant land.

Lot coverage is the area of the **union of clipped structural footprints** divided by parcel land area × 100. It includes the within-boundary portion of straddling footprints and counts overlapping footprints only once. Roads, paved parking, and landscaping are excluded, so this is not impervious-surface coverage. FAR uses whole-contained gross floor area divided by parcel land area; its numerator is unavailable when the outer analysis boundary cuts through a building. Malformed/unusable building geometry makes affected results incomplete rather than zero.

The scorecard separately evaluates the captured baseline parcel set and the materialized proposal parcel set. Only `kind: "parcel"` contributes; block outlines cannot inflate land area. A missing parcel set produces null baseline/proposal values and no unsupported delta. For an aggregate parcel union, FAR can be known even if a building straddles an internal lot boundary; that does not resolve the individual lots' floor-area assignments. The parcel inspector instead compares baseline and proposal buildings within the **same selected boundary**, explicitly labeled as site context rather than a claim that a proposed lot existed in the baseline.

Source metadata distinguishes user proposals (`user-parcel-proposal`) from uploaded boundaries and their optional `sourceFeatureId`; split/assembly lineage retains parent and source IDs. Imported ownership, tax, zoning, and legal-status fields are not inferred or certified. Parcel-bearing projects and history require project format 2, retained after undo; untouched legacy format 1 remains supported. Optional fields are not injected into old captured places, so their canonical hashes remain unchanged. Public snapshots, complete project files, GIS exports, and saved report inputs retain the full applied geometry and provenance.

When neither the captured `parcels` nor scenario `parcelEdits` extension is present, the prior area-only `ParcelEvidence` adapter remains available for compatibility. It uses supplied parcel areas and explicit building parcel IDs, with entered footprint sums rather than spatial footprint unions. A parcel extension selects the geometry-backed method, including unavailable results for an empty parcel set; the two methods are not silently blended. Authoritative national parcel providers, ordinance rules, legal/cadastral reconciliation, and independent validation remain outstanding.

## Implemented with explicit source-backed evidence

The optional `ModelEvidence` interface activates these capabilities without inventing missing national facts:

| Evidence                           | Calculation                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regional direct-effect multipliers | Indirect jobs = direct × (Type I − 1); induced = direct × (Type II − Type I); output from a supplied region/industry output-per-job coefficient       |
| Retail demand/sales                | Eligible recapture capped by unmet demand and added sales capacity, adjusted for local displacement; opportunity gap and establishment-capacity proxy |
| Legacy area-only parcel data       | Aggregate FAR and entered-footprint coverage across supplied parcel areas, using explicit building parcel IDs                                         |
| Regional routing outputs           | 15/30/45-minute car/transit/walk job access; low-income and no-car cohort access                                                                      |
| Environmental evidence             | Canopy area, NRCS curve-number event runoff, supplied relative heat exposure, and mapped flood-exposed units                                          |
| Safety evidence                    | Reviewed pedestrian/bike conflict points, high-injury exposure, emergency travel-time coverage                                                        |
| Explicit sensitivity variants      | Low/expected/high values recomputed using supplied assumption alternatives; these are not statistical confidence intervals                            |

Routing, environmental, and safety evidence must identify the exact scenario revision. Stale evidence is excluded. Regional results retain their economic/routing geography rather than being relabeled as ZIP-local quantities. Input adapters must also preserve source vintage and enforce licensing.

## Captured workforce computation

The separate `resolveWorkforce` module (`workforce-1.0.2`) now aggregates actual captured inbound car/walk route rows against native ACS 2020–2024 education and occupation cells. It is independent of the existing impact scorecard and is independent of global impact model version 0.7.0. Complete state source inventories, exact source-region coverage, graph coverage, native-unit population weights, unallocated geographic splits and every route status are preserved. Education describes residents aged 25+; occupation describes civilian employed residents aged 16+. These are distinct populations, not available applicants.

Conditional sampling margins group source cells within each native unit before applying its reachable weight. Unknown allocation variance is distinguished from missing Census MOEs; known-subtotal margins do not absorb unknown-weight units. Complete totals remain unavailable when contributing evidence is unresolved, while known subtotals and source issues remain inspectable. Input hashes bind the source pack, site/mode, captured graph and actual routing profile, plus independent cohort-coverage proof. [Workforce methodology and source preparation](../../docs/WORKFORCE.md) describe the assumptions, actual data findings and pending site/job/scenario/report integration.

## Outbound workplace accessibility

The standalone `accessibility-1.0.0` method measures travel **from an explicitly selected origin to workplace blocks** on a captured regional network. It is separate from inbound workforce reachability. A saved car or walk result is current-network context for its recorded query, not a baseline/proposal comparison or an area-wide population average. Mode captures retain their own origin and provenance; different origins or networks must not be presented as a controlled mode comparison. Transit, population-weighted equity, and effects of authored planning-road changes require additional routing/data integration.

The retained source is the complete Arizona 2023 LODES WAC `S000/JT01` file. `C000` counts primary jobs at workplaces, not vacancies, available workers, all jobs, or observed trips. Its three earnings cells and twenty industry cells each form a separate partition of that total. They must reconcile independently in every row and must not be added across families. Exact raw cells, physical CSV lines, file checksums, release metadata and row creation dates remain in the private source ledger. A missing, malformed or inconsistent input is rejected rather than coerced to zero. All original rows, including true-zero rows and workplaces in zero-resident-population blocks, remain in the ledger.

Every workplace uses its original 2020 Census block's `INTPTLON20/INTPTLAT20` from the retained TIGER source. The preparation verifies these values against the original DBF. Current block-group split ambiguity does not invalidate the original block point, and no ACS population weights or current-geography allocation is used. This is a complete block-point calculation, not sampling destination blocks or routing to a ZIP/tract centroid. The internal point is nevertheless a representative location, not a verified workplace entrance: all jobs within that block share its modeled network time. Within-block placement and the bounded network snap remain explicit uncertainty.

For threshold `T` of 900, 1,800 or 2,700 seconds, a successful outbound route contributes its whole native block job count exactly when its time is at most `T`. The method retains integer source sums without rounding. A null native route, unsnappable point or unavailable row remains unresolved unless the captured conservative straight-line/max-speed proof independently exceeds that particular threshold. Thus a destination can be proven outside fifteen minutes while remaining unresolved at forty-five minutes. No-route results from a bounded native search are not proof of zero reachable jobs. Zero-job destinations remain known zero without requiring a route.

Full totals require both the exact complete source-region boundary and the regional graph to contain the potential catchment, plus resolved reachability for all positive jobs in the relevant category. Otherwise `estimate` is null. `knownSubtotal` sums resolved contributions; if there are no resolved rows it is null, while resolved zero contributions remain zero. With complete source and graph coverage, `upperBound` adds all unresolved jobs to the known subtotal (or zero when no contribution is resolved). This is an accounting bound, not a confidence interval. Incomplete source/graph coverage withholds that finite upper bound. Category-specific completeness can differ when an unresolved workplace has zero jobs in that category.

Static travel times omit off-network access within the allowed snap gap, parking, congestion and transit schedules. LODES uncertainty and disclosure protection have no supplied ACS-style sampling margin; no MOE is fabricated. Source and graph coverage, routing gaps, representative-point uncertainty and source vintage are reported separately. Every native destination row remains auditable in the archived pack/run. Saved scenario history retains a bounded summary, exact hashes, source inventory and contour reference; it does not duplicate the full route ledger or contour geometry. Imported evidence preserves numbers but carries no server-run authority, and receipts alone do not establish verification.

## Effective FEMA flood-map footprint screening

The optional format-11 flood catalog retains one immutable, content-addressed effective NFHL dataset for an exact study area. A scenario stores a dataset hash, an origin label and an optional calculation-method version; commands and history never duplicate the polygons. An omitted method version means `flood-footprint-1.0.0`. New attachments select `flood-footprint-1.1.0`; updating an older selection is an explicit reversible edit. Existing scenarios, command history and publications retain their original method and source bytes. Original source attributes, per-panel effective dates, later map-revision records, Study_Info revision dates, capture time, source files and before/after capture fences remain distinct. A dataset hash identifies content; it does not authenticate a client-supplied claim of FEMA or server authority. The API separately checks server-catalog references. Restored copies label all retained references imported without changing the source bytes.

Both retained methods intersect the complete Polygon or MultiPolygon structural footprint, including courtyard holes and detached parts, against the retained SFHA polygons. Any positive-area intersection screens **all recorded homes in that building once**. Boundary-only contact contributes no homes. Overlapping hazard polygons are unioned for area context and never multiply a building count. This is an intersecting-building screen, not the locations of individual dwellings, measured flooding, water depth, expected damage, insurance pricing or a legal flood-zone determination. No dwelling count is prorated by intersected area.

A footprint with no SFHA intersection contributes a proven zero only when its entire footprint is covered by known classified map geometry within retained data availability. Zone D, unknown classifications, missing map coverage and geometry errors remain unresolved. A positive SFHA intersection is sufficient for the whole-building screen even if another portion has unknown coverage. Seclusion records remain visible historical-study context and do not erase effective hazard geometry or automatically imply missing data. No area or coordinate tolerance is used to fill map gaps. Method 1.1 first unions the retained SFHA polygons and separately prepares complete classified coverage as `(union(classified zones) ∩ union(availability)) − union(undetermined zones)`. It then intersects or subtracts these prepared regions from each complete footprint. Combining source regions before footprint clipping avoids serializing artificial internal-zone intersections into each building edge. Diagnostic spherical area can cancel at microscopic scales, so an exact BigInt shoelace calculation in the canonical-decimal coordinate domain determines whether an overlay result has positive area; diagnostic square metres never decide the count or completeness.

A conservative method-1.1 shortcut proves strictly inside/outside relationships using indexed boundary segments and exact canonical-decimal point predicates. Any overlap between footprint/source segment bounding boxes falls back to the normal overlay, even when those segments might actually be disjoint. Enclosed holes/islands and all multipart components are checked; mixed or boundary cases also fall back. It does not move a coordinate, snap a point, discard a small gap or accept a configurable epsilon. Derived masks have explicit vertex, polygon and overlay-work bounds; an exceeded bound makes the complete calculation unavailable without truncating source features. Only the required hazard and classified-coverage regions are retained in the per-dataset cache.

Method 1.0 retains its original operation order and numeric results for replay. It clips each source polygon to the footprint before combining coverage, and its residual test treats fewer than three distinct exact points as collapsed. Three-point residual artifacts can therefore keep an older complete total unavailable. Its original SFHA test uses the reported spherical intersection area. These behaviors remain versioned rather than silently rewriting an old report; users can explicitly update the method while retaining the same source map. The current method’s separate exact-area test does not discard microscopic positive overlaps or gaps present in the computed overlay. This is not a claim that finite-precision overlay reconstructs every real-number intersection.

Known zero homes contribute zero even where geographic classification is unresolved. Unknown home counts in a proven-clear building also contribute zero to exposure. Unknown counts in intersecting or unresolved buildings, and known positive homes with unresolved exposure, withhold the full total and delta. The model retains the sum of resolved contributions (null if none), the exact missing-quantity and unclassified building IDs, and each row's nullable contribution. These accounting states are not probability intervals. Building quantities may themselves be user-entered or inferred and are never promoted to observed Census homes.

The baseline and materialized proposal are recomputed against **the same frozen effective hazard dataset**. Added, removed, moved, reshaped and reprogrammed buildings can change exposure. Population, vacancy, growth, road changes and the selected forecast horizon do not change physical home counts or forecast a new hazard map. A different map dataset is an explicit scenario selection, not a silent refresh. Invalid/missing catalog content makes the selected flood metric unavailable and cannot fall back to supplied legacy scalar evidence.

The shared model validates exact content and study-area hashes, indexes immutable map features, and caches footprint classification by exact geometry content. Quantity-only edits reuse geometry but recompute contributions. Mutable caller input is checked before cache reuse. Source admission caps normalized geometry at 50,000 vertices and 8 MiB per pack, with at most eight packs and 16 MiB in a project's catalog; limits reject excessive evidence rather than truncate it. Other existing model paths and legacy project bytes remain unchanged when no flood reference exists.

This completes a bounded effective-map exposure calculation, not hydrology, future climate risk, impervious-surface/runoff modeling, authoritative zoning conformance or safety prediction. Those requirements remain outstanding in the full product plan.

The normalized-dataset **containment validator only** additionally recognizes a tightly bounded binary64 boundary certificate. For each noncollapsed residual component, one common original study-boundary segment must intersect every vertex's closed coordinate cell `[nextDown(x), nextUp(x)] × [nextDown(y), nextUp(y)]`. These endpoints are the immediately adjacent representable doubles, not a chosen metre, degree or area tolerance. Exact dyadic integers and rational segment-parameter intervals prove intersection with `t` within `[0,1]`; the convex one-segment envelope also contains the residual edges/interior. Components spanning unrelated boundary segments or any wider breach are rejected. This explicitly limits the containment claim to binary64 representability, without snapping, changing stored coordinates or claiming exact real-number containment. Actual Chicago clipping residuals exercise the certificate, including a negative `1e-10`-degree breach. The **exposure/coverage calculation does not use this certificate**: genuine areal coverage gaps remain unknown, including very small ones.

The independent source audit separately addresses coordinate-domain differences. The installed JavaScript overlay interprets each Number through its canonical decimal representation; GEOS uses its binary64 value. The audit reconstructs original source/study segment intersections with exact fractions in both domains, checks correct rounding and proves matching complete boundary graphs, ring cycles, components and hole parents. Differing crossings require the same unique original segment pair and incident-edge evidence; exact original collinear subdivisions require on-edge and between-endpoints proofs in both domains. Canonical source/hash formatting is independently verified first. This is an offline QA certificate, not a production geometry repair or a relaxation of footprint coverage. The old failed audit and method-1.0 results remain unchanged. See [the audit implementation, regression cases and limits](../../scripts/qa/flood/README.md).

## Housing affordability: declared housing costs and published income limits

The optional `housing-affordability-1.0.0` method retains HUD Section 8 income-limit packs by canonical content hash in the project, once per pack. Each pack contains the eight published household-size rows, HUD area and county/subdivision components, fiscal year, effective date, exact raw workbook row/cell receipts, source files, and permissions. The published extremely-low, very-low and low limits are used directly. They are not reconstructed as 30%, 50% or 80% of median income: statutory poverty floors, housing-cost adjustments and other HUD rules can make those descriptions numerically different. Section 8 limits are not MTSP/LIHTC or HOME rent limits. Geographic applicability is explicitly reviewed for each group; no ZIP centroid or county-only exception shortcut assigns a HUD area.

Baseline and proposal groups allocate a declared number of actual mapped dwelling units to a building, or to a residential program component. Groups on one side are mutually exclusive. A building may use whole-building assignments or component assignments, but not both. Each group declares tenure, household size from 1 through 8, an income-limit pack, a cost dollar year, and recurring cost components. Bedrooms are descriptive and never converted to household size. This is a comparison for hypothetical households at the selected thresholds, not a census of occupants or a prediction of who will live in the homes. Building units, activity population targets and observed household counts are distinct; activity multipliers do not create additional dwelling inventory here.

Rental groups require rent, utilities and mandatory fees. Ownership groups require loan payment, property tax, property insurance, flood insurance, mortgage insurance, utilities, association/condominium fees, ground rent and mandatory fees. Each component is explicitly separately paid with a nonnegative integer-cent amount, included in a different known separately paid component, not applicable, or unknown. Included utilities, bundled fees or escrow are counted once; inclusion chains and cycles are rejected. An explicit zero or not-applicable amount is different from an unknown amount. The method does not infer a mortgage payment from sale price or invent local utilities, taxes or insurance. Ownership maintenance, transaction costs and down-payment access remain outside this recurring-cost screening unless separately declared within the authored cost scope; this is not a comprehensive homeownership budget or loan underwriting.

For a complete group with monthly cost `C` dollars, required annual gross household income is `12 × C / 0.30`. A group is affordable **at a published annual income limit** `L` when `12 × C ≤ 0.30 × L`, inclusive at equality. The implementation compares integer cents as `2 × monthlyCents ≤ 5 × L`, avoiding a floating-point decision at a published threshold. It does not calculate a household's adjusted income or actual cost burden. Being affordable at the upper limit does not mean being affordable to every lower-income household in that category.

Cumulative ELI/VLI/LI counts overlap and must not be added together. An additional exclusive ledger assigns each evaluated unit to the lowest qualifying threshold, or above the low-income threshold. Costs are never averaged across groups to classify units. Both sides retain total units (null if some quantities are unknown), known units, assigned/evaluated units, known unassigned units, unresolved assigned units, missing building IDs and every original group. Full counts and deltas are null when incomplete; evaluated-group subtotals remain visible. Deleted buildings, missing program components, mixed assignment scopes and overallocated capacity retain the group with a stale reason rather than deleting or silently reallocating it. Ordinary building edits remain permitted.

A review binds the source baseline, materialized building geometry and program/units, full cost groups and citations. Material changes make the review stale and withhold classifications until explicitly renewed. Renaming a scenario or changing unrelated growth assumptions does not reassign housing. All groups in a paired comparison must use the same cost year, and each cost year must match its selected published income-limit fiscal year. There is no automatic inflation, rent growth or selected-horizon extrapolation. A saved project import downgrades all current and historical server-catalog labels to imported while retaining exact packs, costs and review assumptions. Imported content remains explicitly unverified; a content hash alone is not official source authority.

The legacy `housing.incomeRequired` rent-only scalar and projects without affordability evidence retain their existing numbers. New cumulative scorecard rows and `ModelResults.affordability` retain the complete separate method, coverage and source identity for frozen reports and exports. No result establishes subsidy eligibility, a legal rent limit, observed housing cost burden, sufficient housing supply or unmet housing need. Independent housing-program and local cost validation remain necessary for those interpretations.
